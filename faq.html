FAQ

Questions and answers

In brief

Short answers to common questions about the proposal to update Rindge's elderly exemption income limits.

What would the article change?

Only the income limits of the elderly exemption: from $35,000 to $51,000 for a single person and from $49,000 (pending fact check) to $71,500 for a married couple. The exemption amounts ($100,000, $140,000 and $200,000 by age) and the $150,000 asset limit stay the same. It is written to change only the elderly exemption. The proposal

Why those numbers?

They are the 2012 limits brought up to today’s prices with the federal consumer price index. Prices have risen 46.03% since March 2012, so the limits have lost 31.5% of their buying power. The math, step by step

What would it cost me?

The estimate is $21,000 to $63,000 a year in total for the whole town, spread across every taxpayer: about $1.43 to $4.30 a year per $100,000 of assessed value, or roughly $4 to $13 a year on a $300,000 home. Try your home’s value Estimate

Who qualifies today?

Rindge homeowners 65 or older who have lived in New Hampshire for at least 3 consecutive years, with income under the limits and assets (not counting the home) under $150,000. [Source: RSA 72:39-a; Rindge report 2025]

Why did the number of recipients drop?

91 homeowners got the exemption in 2020 and 52 in 2025. The timing points partly to the 2024 review by the town’s assessing firm, which reviewed the exemptions. Year by year

Did the 2025 revaluation leave the town with extra money to pay for this?

This site does not claim that. The town’s share of the property tax went up $437,615 from 2024 to 2025, and the Select Board minutes indicate roughly $400,000 to $500,000 set aside for refunds; how much will be left over is uncertain. The 2025 town tax

Can I sign online?

Please sign on paper. State law doesn't provide for online signatures on a petition like this, and the town isn't required to accept them. Where to sign, or request a home visit.

How many signatures are needed, and by when?

State law requires the signatures of 25 registered voters. Petitions are due Tuesday, Jan. 12, 2027. [Source: RSA 39:3; RSA 40:13]

Can the article be changed before the vote?

Yes. At the deliberative session, a single session held between Jan. 30 and Feb. 6, 2027, on a date the Select Board sets, voters can amend it, up or down, but not remove its subject. The amended version goes on the Tuesday, March 9, 2027 ballot. [Source: RSA 40:13, III, IV]

If it passes, when would it take effect?

No later than the tax year starting April 1, 2027. [Source: RSA 72:27-a, II] Homeowners would apply by April 15, 2027. [Source: RSA 72:27-a; RSA 72:33]

Is signing up for a home visit the same as signing?

No. This is not a signature. We’ll bring the paper petition to you. Listing on the supporter wall is also not a signature.
