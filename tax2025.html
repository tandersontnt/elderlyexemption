2025 town tax

The 2025 revaluation and the town’s tax

In brief

The revaluation nearly doubled assessed values, so the tax rate fell. The town’s share of the tax still went up.

  • 91.2%Increase in net assessed valuation, 2024 to 2025

The town tax and the refund reserve

Some will ask what this costs. After the 2025 revaluation, the town’s share of the property tax rose about $440,000 over the year before ($437,615: $3,577,373 in 2025 against $3,139,758 in 2024), even though the amount voted for spending went down (about $5.64 million in 2025, down from about $5.85 million in 2024). [Source: Rindge report 2024; Rindge report 2025; calculation]

Select Board minutes indicate much of it, roughly $400,000 to $500,000, was set aside as an “overlay” to cover abatement refunds. [Source: Select Board minutes, Oct. 8, 2025; Select Board minutes, Oct. 22, 2025] State law allows an overlay “to answer any abatements that may be made,” paid into the town treasury “for the use of the town.” [Source: RSA 76:6]

Whether any of it is left over depends on abatements still being settled. At one point a selectman warned the overlay might not be enough. [Source: Select Board minutes, Oct. 22, 2025, p.3] If there is a leftover, it goes back into the town’s General Fund [Source: Select Board minutes, Oct. 22, 2025, p.2], and the Select Board could use it to hold down the rate. The state’s tax-rate form has a line for “fund balance to reduce taxes.” [Source: Rindge report 2020, pdf p.63]

Any leftover could help offset a change that shifts, at most, about $63,000 a year across all taxpayers combined. The estimated range is about $21,000 to $63,000 for the whole town, or roughly $4 to $13 a year on a $300,000 home. [Source: estimate]

The extra $440,000 is tax that was raised, not money the town has to spare, and any leftover may be small or nothing. The spending totals were added up from the town reports; they are not an official state total. Some 2024 figures were read from scanned pages.
