How to sign

A resident’s guide to the March 2027 petition · Draft, not yet filed

The proposal

One change: the income limits

In brief

From $35,000 to $51,000 for a single person, and from $49,000 to $71,500 for a married couple. Nothing else changes.

Draft: This article has not been filed and has not been reviewed by a lawyer, the town or the state. It is not legal advice. The wording may change before signatures are collected.

The draft article

ARTICLE __. (By Petition) Shall the Town of Rindge vote, under RSA 72:27-a and RSA 72:39-b, to modify the elderly exemption from property tax, adopted under RSA 72:39-a and 72:39-b, by increasing the maximum net income to qualify from $35,000 to $51,000 for a single person and from $49,000 to $71,500 for married persons (combined net income), so that the elderly exemption shall be as follows: based on assessed value, for qualified taxpayers, $100,000 for a person 65 years of age up to 75 years; $140,000 for a person 75 years of age up to 80 years; and $200,000 for a person 80 years of age or older; to qualify, the taxpayer must have a net income of not more than $51,000 or, if married, a combined net income of not more than $71,500, and own net assets not in excess of $150,000 or, if married, combined net assets not in excess of $150,000, excluding the value of the person’s residence; “net income,” “net assets,” “residence,” and all other qualifications to be as provided in RSA 72:39-a and 72:39-b; the exemption amounts and net asset limits being unchanged by this article; and this modification to take effect on the date set by the Select Board or in the tax year beginning April 1, 2027, whichever occurs first, as provided in RSA 72:27-a, II? (By Petition)

Laws cited: [Source: RSA 72:27-a; RSA 72:39-a; RSA 72:39-b]. The article passes with a simple majority. [Source: RSA 40:13, XIII]

What voters approved before

The last two changes, quoted from the warrants printed in the town reports:

“Shall the town vote to increase the income limit amount for the disabled and elderly exemption from $25,000 to $35,000 for a single person and $35,000 to $49,000 for married couples with no changes in the current asset limit amount? (By Petition)”

2012 Article 20. Passed 615 to 492. [Source: Rindge report 2011, pdf p.45] [Source: Rindge report 2012]

“Shall the town vote to increase the exemption amounts for the elderly, which shall be subtracted each year from the assessment on the elderly person’s residential property prior to calculating the tax, from $50,000 to $100,000 for persons ages 65-74, from $70,000 to $140,000 for persons ages 75-79, and from $100,000 to $200,000 for persons ages 80 and over; further, to increase the income limits from $18,940 to $25,000 for single persons and from $27,072 to $35,000 for married couples; and further, to increase the asset limit from $50,000 to $150,000?”

2006 Article 27, as printed in the 2005 report. The posted article proposed an asset limit of $65,000; the Feb. 11, 2006 deliberative session amended it to $150,000. Passed 1,267 to 182. [Source: Rindge report 2005, pdf p.17] [Source: Rindge report 2006, pdf pp.73-74]

Petition rules

  • Who can sign: registered Rindge voters.
  • How many: State law requires the signatures of 25 registered voters. [Source: RSA 39:3] The goal is to gather 40 to 50 and file well ahead of the deadline. [Source: organizer's plan]
  • Deadline: Tuesday, Jan. 12, 2027, the second Tuesday in January for towns that use an official ballot. The town still has to confirm this date. [Source: RSA 40:13, II-a(b)]

Handing it in

Petitions go to the Select Board or any one member. We aim to hand them in by Tuesday, Jan. 5, 2027, a week before the Tuesday, Jan. 12, 2027 deadline. We will keep a copy and get a dated receipt. The town clerk may check that each signer is a registered voter. [Source: RSA 39:3; organizer's plan]

The numbers will be rechecked. The figures use August 2026 prices, the latest published. If the September 2026 CPI-U (due mid-October) is 335.895 or higher, the rounded limits become $51,500 and $72,000. The numbers will be fixed before any signatures are collected. [Source: BLS CPI-U; calculation]

The cost, honestly Estimate

  • $4–$13 a yearOn a $300,000 home: the estimated added tax for one householdEstimate
  • $1.43–$4.30A year for each $100,000 of assessed valueEstimate
  • $21,000–$63,000A year in total for the whole town, spread across every taxpayer. This is not a per-taxpayer figure.Estimate

Every exemption shifts a little tax onto everyone else, and voters deserve a straight answer about how much. My best estimate is $21,000 to $63,000 a year in total for the whole town, spread across every taxpayer. That works out to about $1.43 to $4.30 a year for each $100,000 of assessed value, or roughly $4 to $13 a year on a $300,000 home. The cost of each added person, about $1,740 a year, comes from the town’s own 2025 figures. What no one knows exactly is how many more neighbors would qualify, because there is no public income data for Rindge’s older homeowners. The range assumes about 12 to 36 more people. [Source: Cost calculation in the author's guest column; estimate; Rindge report 2025]

About 17.7% of any added tax is town tax (town rate $2.44 of the $13.79 total per $1,000 in 2025); the rest is school and county tax. [Source: Municipal Tax Rates]

Please treat this as an estimate, not a promise. Middle estimate: $2.86 per $100,000 of assessed value.
