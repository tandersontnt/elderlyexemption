Timeline

Fifty years of the elderly exemption

In brief

Every change to the exemption came from a town vote. Every town vote on the exemption is listed. Filled squares mark the five votes cited most often; hatched squares are 2027 dates still ahead. Figures with a dotted underline are pending fact check.

Events, oldest first

  1. March 2, 1976

    Rindge adopts the exemption

    Article 39 adopts the state’s “Expanded Elderly” exemption, Yes 224 to No 77. [Source: Rindge report 1975; Rindge report 1976]

  2. March 14, 1978

    Asset limit raised

    Article 36 raises the asset limit from $35,000 to $50,000. It passes 324 to 88. [Source: Rindge report 1977; Rindge report 1978]

  3. March 11, 1980

    Based on actual assessed value

    Article 28 changes the basis from equalized to actual assessed value. It passes 317 to 52. [Source: Rindge report 1980]

  4. March 8, 1983

    Adjusted exemption

    Article 19 sets exemptions of $10,000, $15,000 and $20,000 of assessed value by age, for income under $10,000 single or $12,000 married, with an asset limit of $30,000 not counting the home, and 5 years of New Hampshire residency. It passes 494 to 87. [Source: Rindge report 1982; Rindge report 1983]

  5. March 13, 1990

    Big increase, by petition

    Article 27 raises the exemptions to $50,000, $70,000 and $100,000. It passes 425 to 106. [Source: Rindge report 1989; Rindge report 1990]

  6. March 11, 1997

    Re-adopted under the new state law

    Article 6 moves the exemption to RSA 72:39-a and 72:39-b (the article still required 5 years of residency; state law now sets 3). A floor amendment raises the income limits to $18,940 and $27,072; the asset limit is $50,000. It passes 185 to 43. [Source: Rindge report 1996; Rindge report 1997]

  7. March 14, 2006

    Doubled after the revaluation

    After the 2005 revaluation roughly doubled home values on average, Article 27 doubles the exemptions to today’s $100,000 (ages 65 to 74), $140,000 (75 to 79) and $200,000 (80 and older). It raises the income limits to $25,000 and $35,000, and the asset limit to $150,000. The posted article proposed an asset limit of $65,000; the Feb. 11, 2006 deliberative session amended it to $150,000. It passes 1,267 to 182. [Source: Rindge report 2005; Rindge report 2006, 2006 rpt pdf pp.73-74]

    See the 2006 minutes

  8. March 13, 2012

    Today’s income limits are set

    A citizens’ petition, Article 20, raises the income limits to $35,000 single and $49,000 married. It passes 615 to 492. These are still the limits today. [Source: Rindge report 2011; Rindge report 2012]

  9. 2020

    91 recipients

    The town’s 2020 MS-1 report lists 91 homeowners receiving the exemption. [Source: Rindge report 2020, pdf p.70]

  10. Jan. 30, 2021

    A proposed cut becomes a study committee

    Article 19 proposed cutting the limits. Amended into a study committee at deliberative session (18 to 6). Limits unchanged. [Source: Rindge report 2020; Rindge report 2021, 2021 rpt pdf p.25]

  11. March 8, 2022

    Voters reject the cut

    The same cut came back by petition as Article 11. Voters rejected it, 601 No to 395 Yes. [Source: Rindge report 2022, pdf p.75]

  12. 2024

    The exemptions are reviewed

    The town’s new assessing firm reviewed the elderly exemptions. The drop in recipients points partly to this review. [Source: Rindge report 2024, pdf p.9]

  13. 2025

    52 recipients

    The 2025 MS-1 lists 52 recipients (20 ages 65 to 74, 13 ages 75 to 79, 19 age 80 and older) and $6,559,100 of assessed value exempted. [Source: Rindge report 2025, pdf p.91]

  14. Tuesday, Jan. 12, 2027

    Petition deadline

    The last day to hand in petitioned articles. [Source: RSA 40:13, II-a(b); town to confirm]

  15. Jan. 30 to Feb. 6, 2027

    Deliberative session

    A single session held between Jan. 30 and Feb. 6, 2027, on a date the Select Board sets. Voters there can discuss and amend the article. [Source: RSA 40:13, III and IV]

  16. Tuesday, March 9, 2027

    Town vote

    The article passes with more than half of the votes. [Source: RSA 40:13, VII and XIII]

  17. April 1, 2027

    New limits would apply

    If it passes, the new limits apply no later than the tax year that starts April 1, 2027. Homeowners would apply by April 15, 2027. [Source: RSA 72:27-a, II] [Source: RSA 72:33]

Page numbers marked ~ in the sources are approximate. Details for each event are on the Sources page.
