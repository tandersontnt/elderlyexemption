The law

The legal basis

In brief

State law sets the rules and the minimums. Each town sets its own amounts and limits by a town vote. Excerpts below are quoted from the official text; the full sections are linked, and the official pages control.

RSA 72:39-a: who qualifies

“Has resided in this state for at least 3 consecutive years preceding April 1 in the year in which the exemption is claimed.”

“… a net income from all sources, or if married, a combined net income from all sources, of not more than the respective amount … determined by the city or town … Under no circumstances shall the amount determined by the city or town be less than $13,400 for a single person or $20,400 for married persons.”

“Owns … net assets not in excess of the amount determined by the city or town … excluding the value of the person’s actual residence and the land upon which it is located up to the greater of 2 acres or the minimum single family residential lot size … The amount determined by the city or town shall not be less than $35,000.”

[Source: RSA 72:39-a, I(a)-(c)]

RSA 72:39-b: the amounts, set by the town

“A town or city may adopt or modify elderly exemptions by the procedure in RSA 72:27-a.”

“An elderly exemption, based on assessed value for qualified taxpayers, may be granted for a different dollar amount determined by the town or city, to a person 65 years of age up to 75 years, to a person 75 years of age up to 80 years, and to a person 80 years of age or older. … Under no circumstances shall the amounts of the exemption for any age category be less than $5,000.”

[Source: RSA 72:39-b, I-II]

RSA 72:27-a: how a town changes it

“… the question shall be placed on the warrant of a special or annual town meeting, by the governing body or by petition pursuant to RSA 39:3.”

“If a majority of those voting on the question vote ‘yes,’ the exemption or credit shall take effect … on the date set by the governing body, or in the tax year beginning April 1 following its adoption, whichever shall occur first.”

“A municipality may modify, if applicable, or rescind the exemption … in the manner described in this section.”

[Source: RSA 72:27-a, I(a), II, III]

RSA 39:3: petitioned articles

“Upon the written application of 25 or more registered voters or 2 percent of the registered voters in town, whichever is less, although in no event shall fewer than 10 registered voters be sufficient … the selectmen shall insert in their warrant for such meeting the petitioned article with only such minor textual changes as may be required.”

“The written application … may identify one of the registered voters on the application as the primary petitioner. If no primary petitioner is identified, the primary petitioner shall be the first registered voter listed on the written application.”

State law requires the signatures of 25 registered voters. [Source: RSA 39:3]

RSA 40:13: official-ballot (SB 2) town meetings

“… the final date for submission of petitioned articles under RSA 39:3 … shall be the second Tuesday in January …” (II-a(b))

“Warrant articles may be amended at the first session, subject to the following limitations: … No warrant article shall be amended to eliminate the subject matter of the article.” (IV)

“Warrant articles that are amended shall be placed on the official ballot for a final vote on the main motion, as amended.” (IV(b))

[Source: RSA 40:13, II-a(b), IV]

Session dates: “The first session of the annual meeting … shall be held between the first and second Saturdays following the last Monday in January, inclusive of those Saturdays … at a time prescribed by the local political subdivision’s governing body.” For 2027 that is a single session held between Jan. 30 and Feb. 6, 2027, on a date the Select Board sets. [Source: RSA 40:13, III]
